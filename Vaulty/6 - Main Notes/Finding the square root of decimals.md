# Finding the square root of decimals

When you are doing something like $\sqrt{3.24}$ you need to figure out 2 things. 

Step 1: Change it to a whole number and remember how many multiples of 10 you used to get there. So in this case we take $\sqrt{3.24} \times 100 = \sqrt{324}$ 
Step 2: then you want to prime factorise it to find groups of 2. $\sqrt{324} = \sqrt{2 \times 162} = \sqrt{2 \times 2 \times 81} =  \sqrt{2 \times 2 \times 9 \times 9}$
Step 3: Simply the square root $2 \times 9 = 18$ 
Step 4: Remember the $\times 100$ at the beginning? Well now we need to use it and work out what to do. When you have a something like $\sqrt{3.24}$ you need to know you're multiplying 2 numbers together. So the 2 numbers need to share between the 2 decimal places found in the $3.24$. Meaning each number need to have **ONE** decimal between them to get a 2 decimal place number.
Meaning that $1.8 \times 1.8 = 3.24$

##### Finding the square root of decimals
##### Reference - https://www.khanacademy.org/math/cc-eighth-grade-math/cc-8th-numbers-operations/cc-8th-roots/v/finding-square-root-of-decimal

When you are doing something like $\sqrt{3.24}$ you need to figure out 2 things. 

Step 1: Change it to a whole number and remember how many multiples of 10 you used to get there. So in this case we take $\sqrt{3.24} \times 100 = \sqrt{324}$ 
Step 2: then you want to prime factorise it to find groups of 2. $\sqrt{324} = \sqrt{2 \times 162} = \sqrt{2 \times 2 \times 81} =  \sqrt{2 \times 2 \times 9 \times 9}$
Step 3: Simply the square root $2 \times 9 = 18$ 
Step 4: Remember the $\times 100$ at the beginning? Well now we need to use it and work out what to do. When you have a something like $\sqrt{3.24}$ you need to know you're multiplying 2 numbers together. So the 2 numbers need to share between the 2 decimal places found in the $3.24$. Meaning each number need to have **ONE** decimal between them to get a 2 decimal place number.
Meaning that $1.8 \times 1.8 = 3.24$

##### Rate and proportional relationships
This is all to do with linear relationships between an input variable and an output variable, so this is all straight line stuff. This is all straight line equation stuff.

First thing to note is that for a given unit you have ratios between then when you have a linear relation. For example in khan academy question when it says 

Selena's favorite chocolate chip cookie recipe calls for $1$ teaspoon of salt for every ${2 \dfrac{1}{4}}$ cups flour.

The units here are cups of flour ${2 \dfrac{1}{4}}$ and  $1$ teaspoon of salt so the ratio between then can be through of ${2 \dfrac{1}{4}}$ :  $1$ which reads for every ${2 \dfrac{1}{4}}$ bags of flour you have  $1$ teaspoon of salt.

I state this so explictly because you have multiply both sides by any number to make more sense of them so if I take 
$${2 \dfrac{1}{4}} :1  = {\dfrac{9}{4}} : 1$$
Then multiply it by four we would get.
$$ \big({\dfrac{9}{4}} : 1 \big) \times 4 = 9 : 4$$
